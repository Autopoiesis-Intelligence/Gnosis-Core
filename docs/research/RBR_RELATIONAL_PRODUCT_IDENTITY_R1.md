## 1. Purpose

This branch treats product rebranding as a research experiment rather than a cosmetic naming exercise.

The current product has evolved beyond a system whose primary description is simply "knowledge" or "gnosis". The present architecture studies constrained evolution, verification, reflection, persistence, trust boundaries, environment interaction and the formation of relations.

The experiment asks:

> Can product identity be studied as something that emerges from the structure of relations the product forms, preserves, tests and develops?

The experiment does **not** assume that the product is conscious, sentient, autonomous in a metaphysical sense, or a social subject. Those are outside the engineering claim boundary.

## 3. Core hypothesis

### H-RL-001 — Relational Product Identity

A product's durable identity may be better characterized by the stable structure of relations it forms and maintains than by a static label describing one of its capabilities.

Working model:

`Product Identity ≈ stable relational organization + capabilities + constraints + provenance`

This is a research hypothesis, not an established law.

## 4. Relational learning experiment

The experiment treats the development environment as a set of interacting entities and boundaries:

`Researcher ↔ Research Instrument ↔ Product ↔ External World`

The relevant object is not merely the output of any one participant, but the evolution of relations among them.

Candidate relation classes:

- researcher ↔ product;
- product ↔ research instrument;
- product ↔ external evidence;
- product ↔ other agents;
- product ↔ tools;
- internal component ↔ internal component;
- current state ↔ historical lineage;
- hypothesis ↔ counterexample;
- tension ↔ new distinction;
- observation ↔ model;
- capability ↔ authorization;
- identity ↔ transformation.

For each relation, research should ask:

1. What entities participate?
2. What information crosses the boundary?
3. What constraints govern the relation?
4. What can change?
5. What remains invariant?
6. What evidence demonstrates the relation?
7. Can the relation produce a new capability or distinction?
8. Can the relation fail or become adversarial?
9. Is the relation stable across state transitions?
10. Does the observed relation generalize beyond the particular experiment?

## 5. OpenAI as research instrument

OpenAI/ChatGPT may be used as a research and development instrument in this experiment.

The distinction is mandatory:

`Research Instrument ≠ Product Owner`

`AI Output ≠ Automatically Validated Product Knowledge`

`AI Tool ≠ Gnozis Architecture`

The use of OpenAI technology does not make the product an OpenAI product, and the product must remain architecturally independent of any particular model/provider.

The experiment may therefore compare or replace research instruments without changing the identity of the research object.

Potential instruments include multiple independent AI systems, human analysis, executable tests, external evidence and other research tools.

## 8. Product identity experiment

The rebranding question is deliberately left open.

### B. Evolution

Evidence, invariants, integrity, bounded trust.

- relation-centric direction;
- technical independence;
- linguistic clarity;

- external naming collisions;

## 11. Falsification / adversarial tests

The relational-identity hypothesis should be challenged by at least:

1. A product whose capabilities change but whose relation structure remains stable.
2. A product with many interactions but no stable relational organization.
3. Relations that are merely interface artifacts.
4. Relations created by the researcher but incorrectly attributed to the product.
5. Apparent autonomous relation formation caused entirely by external orchestration.
6. Relations that disappear after restart or loss of context.
7. Relations that cannot be reproduced or independently observed.
8. Relations that improve local behavior while degrading system-level properties.
9. Provider-specific behavior incorrectly interpreted as product identity.
10. Human conversational interpretation incorrectly treated as evidence of product agency.

A failed test is research evidence and must be retained with provenance.

## 13. Experimental outcome criteria

The experiment becomes informative if it can demonstrate, with reproducible evidence, one or more of:

- stable relation structures across product transformations;
- relation-dependent learning behavior;
- formation of new distinctions from interaction;
- preservation of useful relational invariants across state transitions;
- measurable difference between externally imposed interaction and product-generated relation selection;
- emergence of a more accurate product description than the historical "knowledge" framing.

None of these criteria alone establishes consciousness, personhood or metaphysical autonomy.
